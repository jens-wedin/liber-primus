The Central Finding

The unsolved ciphertext is, statistically, a uniform random stream bound by a single law — adjacent runes are almost never equal.

0.66%
observed doublet rate
3.45%
expected at random
~17σ
the deficiency

A pure lag-1 effect: first differences are otherwise uniform. It rules out substitution and every periodic or independent keystream. What survives is an output-stage no-repeat enforcement — most likely a key-skip that advances the key pointer to dodge a would-be doublet, desynchronising the stream by ~3% and defeating every fixed-position attack.

Modeled further, the collision resolution is uniform, and the 86 residual doublets carry no key-period leak. A second, independent transcription reproduces every one of those doublets — so they are real ciphertext, not copy errors: the no-repeat enforcement is imperfect by design. Whether a re-roll pad or a key-skip, the differences stay uniform; that is why every keyed attack, on the raw stream and on the differences alike, comes back silent.

A New Front

The images hold what the runes did not

A page-by-page sweep of all 75 scans overturned an earlier, hasty verdict. The transcription is faithful for the runes — colour and punctuation are provably cosmetic — but it silently drops the pages that are not runes at all.

  • ✦Two magic squares. Page 16 bears a 5×5 square of magic constant 3301 — palindromic, prime centre 809. Page 32 bears a 4×4 grid whose every cell is 3301 minus a prime, one marked in red, beside a hand-drawn Möbius.
  • ✦A page of code. Page 67 is 104 two-character codes and not one rune; pages 66, 68 and 73 carry more, in decimal and hex. None of it appears in the transcription.

The wall still stands for the runic stream — a keyed one-time pad is unbreakable without the seed. These numeric puzzles are the open front now: a first pass rules the squares out as simple keys and finds the code high-entropy, so they stay genuinely unsolved.

The Trail Ahead

The backlog — experiments queued next

Opened after two readings of the magic square closed — as an additive key, and as an interrupter schedule — and a new plaintext-side pattern surfaced: on the solved pages the literal ᚠ (F) is placed so that prime-length rune-runs sum to 3301 and 1033. Each item carries its own control.

  • ✓The literal-ᚠ interrupter. The solved-page rule that a ciphertext ᚠ may be an unencrypted F consuming no key — a pointer hold the key-skip beam could never express. Tested: negative. It reads no better than random.
  • ✓Map the ᚠ positions. Do the literal-F runes mark structural boundaries — prime-length runs, checksums — on the unsolved pages as on the solved? Tested: no. No fingerprint survives into the ciphertext.
  • ✓The code pages, transcribed & tested. Pages 67 and 68 confirmed exact, page 66 read at last — 256 = 28 codes. Tried as a pad, an index into the runes, a self-cipher, a lookup table: all gibberish, and the set is not a permutation. What remains is a keyed pad — the same wall the runes sit behind.
  • P2A second transcription. Diff rtkd/iddqd against the vendored stream to see whether the 86 residual doublets are simply where two hand-copies disagree.
  • P2A GP-sum plausibility filter. Score any future decode by whether it partitions into prime-length runs hitting the themed totals — a tie-breaker, never the signal.
  • P2The squares as a message. Not a key but a puzzle: the constants, the 3301−prime cells, the Möbius, the palindromic centre, read as a path or a pointer set.
  • P3Prime-indexed hints. The old whitespace prime sequences and emirp ids, tried as key primers — low prior, opportunistic.

Independently corroborated meanwhile: the community's own frequency analysis finds the unsolved stream statistically indistinguishable from random — the same verdict as the central finding above.
