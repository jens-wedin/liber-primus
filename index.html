The Central Finding

The unsolved ciphertext is, statistically, a uniform random stream bound by a single law — adjacent runes are almost never equal.

0.66%

~17σ
the deficiency

A pure lag-1 effect: first differences are otherwise uniform. It rules out substitution and every periodic or independent keystream. What survives is an output-stage no-repeat enforcement — most likely a key-skip that advances the key pointer to dodge a would-be doublet, desynchronising the stream by ~3% and defeating every fixed-position attack.

Modeled further, the collision resolution is uniform and the 86 residual doublets are transcription noise — no key-period leak. Whether a re-roll pad or a key-skip, the differences stay uniform; that is why every keyed attack, on the raw stream and on the differences alike, comes back silent.

What Remains

The transcription holds no more

The page images have now been read against the transcription. The features it drops — the red illuminated capitals, the stray punctuation — are provably cosmetic: solved pages carry them and still decode exactly. So the input every attack ran on is faithful, and the scans add nothing to the 12,956-rune stream.

  • ✦One curiosity. A few pages carry small non-rune glyphs the transcription omits — cuneiform-like marks at the foot of page 50 — sitting outside the cipher stream. Ornament, or a small cipher of their own.

The wall. If the keystream is a keyed CSPRNG pad, then c = p + k is a genuine one-time pad — unbreakable without the seed. That is precisely why c is uniform, and why no attack over the transcription can beat it.

Every avenue over the transcription — and now the images too — is exhausted and control-validated. The realistic value here was always a rigorously narrowed hypothesis space, not a break; that is, in good conscience, now complete.
