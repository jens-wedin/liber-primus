CONCLUSION
At L=2 the true key does not even rank #1 (~5 wrong 2-rune keys score higher):
the key-skip desync gives the decoder enough per-position freedom to bend many
wrong short keys toward English. L=3-4 reach rank ~1 only with a longer head,
and even then the ~1/4500 per-key false-positive rate, scaled over a full
29^3 = 24,389-key brute, implies several chance "hits". Since brute force is
only feasible at L=2-3 and clean identifiability only arrives at L>=5 (29^5 =
20.5M, not brute-forceable), a very-short-key brute + key-skip cannot decisively
test short keys. This is the same intrinsic underpowerment as the key-text-free
running-key attack (REPORT.md §4) — a decoder with too much freedom fits any
input. A full L=3 brute was therefore NOT run; it would return chance decodes,
not a clean negative.
